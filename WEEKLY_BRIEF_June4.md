> **"The $122.55 difference is not the cost of better food. It is the cost of the system that was previously absent."**

When I tracked every expenditure for three months, I discovered something: the problem was not that I was buying too much. The problem was that I had no system preventing me from buying too much.

The envelope method is that system.

---

## How It Works (The 30-Second Version)

1. **Divide your weekly budget into five categories** (Grains, Protein, Vegetables, Dairy, Misc)
2. **Plan your meals for the week** (Sunday, 30 minutes)
3. **Make one shopping trip** (don't go back mid-week)
4. **Track spending daily** (5 minutes, just numbers)
5. **Adjust next week** if you need to

That's it. The discipline is in the structure, not in willpower.

## If You're Stuck

**Common questions (and answers):**

**Q: What if I have less than $300/week?**
A: The system adapts. Divide $300 by five envelopes. Plan simpler meals. But the principle holds — one budget, five categories, one trip, daily tracking.

**Q: What if my children are picky eaters?**
A: Adapt the meal plan to their preferences. The envelope still works. It might cost slightly more, but you'll still be on budget.

**Q: What if I have irregular income?**
A: Reset each Sunday with what you actually have. Bad week? Tighter meal plan. Good week? You have breathing room. The weekly reset is built in.

**Q: This sounds too rigid. What if something unexpected happens?**
A: It will. Kennedy gets sick. Oil runs out mid-week. The meal plan needs adjustment. That's not system failure. That's real life. Adapt. Track. Move forward. The envelope method survives real life because it's not a prison — it's a boundary.
